## 1. YouTube packaging and long-form production (MrBeast memo, Paddy Galloway, Ali Abdaal, title/thumbnail-first, outlier research, retention editing)

### Takeaway
The best-documented long-form method is packaging-first: title + thumbnail (+ hook) are fixed before any scripting, and the video is built in timed retention segments (first minute, ~min 3 re-engagement, ~min 6 re-engagement, abrupt payoff ending). Ideas are picked by outlier evidence (videos running 2x to 10x+ above their own channel's baseline) and by side-by-side one-page comparison. YouTube's native A/B test now picks winners by watch-time share, not CTR, so the agent should score packaging on "click + stay", not click alone.

### Cited Findings

**MrBeast "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" memo** — [PRIMARY document, hosted copy; authenticity NOT officially confirmed; page © 2025, images dated 2024-09; memo itself undated] — [Alexander Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Three metrics staff must care about: **CTR** (clicks ÷ thumbnail impressions), **AVD** (average view duration), **AVP** (average view percentage). AVP matters less for production staff because video length is set by leadership. — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Every team member must know the title and thumbnail of the video they work on. "The title and thumbnail... set the expectations for the viewer." Mismatched expectations cause click-off. — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Titles should feel more extreme. The memo's example: "I Survived" beats "I Spent". — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." The memo's levers for cutting early drop-off are good lighting, matching the clickbait, pre-planned dialogue, and front-loading the interesting content. Its example is a video that lost 21M viewers in the first minute. — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Minutes 1–3: "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them." Use "crazy progression" (e.g., cover several days early in a survival video). — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- ~Minute 3: a "re-engagement", meaning a highly interesting, on-story segment. ~Minutes 3–6: simple, exciting content with quick scene changes. ~Minute 6: another re-engagement that pushes the story forward. Minute 6 to the end: weaker back halves "have killed videos". Don't signal the ending early. End abruptly on a satisfying payoff. — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Other rules: avoid dull moments. "BRAND DEALS ARE CONTENT!" (integrated deals reduce retention dips). Show money being spent on camera. "The simpler the better." Don't run the same format back to back; expect to keep "constantly ditching formats". "Wow factor" (something no other YouTuber can do) is subjective and can't be tracked. — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Numbers in the memo: average length of the last 100 videos was 818 s (~13m37s). One example video had ~1m38s more average watch time than a similar-length video and got ~3x the views (120M vs 45M). — [Jarvis copy](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Authenticity caveat: secondary coverage says the document "hasn't been confirmed as authentic" and attributes the leak to Rosanna Pansino. An Audible listing titled "How2 Youtube" credited to Jimmy Donaldson also exists, which may mean an official version was released later (not verified). — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/); [Audible listing](https://www.audible.com/pd/B0G8SWVJRZ)

**Paddy Galloway** — [SECONDARY: Orbit newsletter, 2024-11-01, summarizing his conversation with Jay Clouse]
- One-page idea method. For each idea, one sheet holds (1) a rough thumbnail sketch, (2) a working title, (3) 2–3 reasons the idea should work, and (4) a logline. "Once you've filled the page, you'll instantly get a feel for whether this idea has potential or belongs in the trash." Then put two pages side by side (two versions of one idea, or two different ideas), and share the page for quick feedback. — [Orbit #62](https://orbitforcreators.beehiiv.com/p/issue62)
- A Creator Science "Greatest Hits #152" episode with Galloway ("The most sought-after YouTube consultant on the planet") is dated 2024-02-09 (re-release). Transcript not retrieved. — [Creator Science listing](https://podwise.ai/podcasts/389?page=7)

**Ali Abdaal** — [PRIMARY transcript via third-party transcript site; video date not shown]
- Stated order: idea → **title, thumbnail, and hook** → bullet-point outline → film → edit (outsourced) → publish/analytics. "do not even think about writing the video unless you know what the title, thumbnail, and hook are going to be." The thumbnail can be a clear concept, not a finished design. The hook is the first 30 seconds. — [Sozai transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/make-youtube-videos-faster-ali-abdaal/)
- For educational videos he advises bullet points instead of a word-for-word script. Full scripting took him ~12 h per video, and the teleprompter made him stumble. "you cannot be editing your own videos unless you really, really, really love it." He uses a thumbnail photo library and a consistent house style. — [Sozai transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/make-youtube-videos-faster-ali-abdaal/)
- Secondhand: the title and thumbnail should promise the same outcome. — [Alici thumbnail playbook (2026, SECONDARY)](https://alici.ai/blog/ali-abdaal-thumbnail-playbook-2026)

**Outlier-based idea research**
- [PRIMARY tool doc, vidIQ, n.d.] "An outlier is a video that has surpassed a channel's usual performance." The score is "a video's performance relative to the average performance of other videos on the same channel." Color bands: <2x, 2–5x, 5–10x, >10x. Filters cover outlier score, views, subscribers, views/hour, length, and publish date. Workflow: pick a precise topic → filter for high outlier scores → widen the filters if results are sparse → "Give me ideas" / "Find similar videos" / "Add as competitor". — [vidIQ Outliers help](https://support.vidiq.com/en/articles/9660010-outliers)
- [SECONDARY] Baseline critique: dividing by the channel average ignores channel size. One tool normalizes views per subscriber against the median ratio of the channel's own videos. — [ytgrowth.io](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-outlier-video)
- [SECONDARY] 1of10 is categorized as a pure outlier-discovery tool, with "partial" transparency about its method. Its formula is not confirmed. — [Overseeros comparison](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/best-youtube-outlier-tools)

**YouTube native packaging tests** — [SECONDARY coverage; official Help article not retrieved]
- Test & Compare lets you upload up to 3 thumbnails, and title testing was added (rollout reported ~Sept 2025). The winner is chosen by **watch time share** ("watch time per impression"), not CTR alone. vidIQ (updated July 2026) says it is now called "A/B test titles & thumbnails", supports Title only, Thumbnail only, or Title+thumbnail, usually finishes within 2 weeks, and labels results winner / performed the same / inconclusive. — [Thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/); [Routenote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-expand-test-compare-how-to-use-it/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-launches-new-thumbnail-testing-tool/)

**Monetization policy that constrains AI-automated channels** — [PRIMARY, YouTube Help, update dated 2025-07-15]
- The policy was renamed from "repetitious content" to "**inauthentic content**". It covers content that is repetitive or mass-produced. The reused-content policy is unchanged. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Not allowed: "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Allowed: the same intro/outro if "the bulk of your content is different". Using AI "to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual" is allowed. With automated tools, "the final product must still demonstrate your creative vision." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Reused content: "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary" is OK. "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative" is not. This "applies even if you have permission from the original creator." — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- Press framing differs. Some outlets call it a clarification ("for most creators, nothing will change"). Others call it an aggressive crackdown on AI slop. — [Plagiarism Today 2025-07-08](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)

### Inferences
- **Encodable stage gate G0 (Idea → Package):** no script is generated until a "Package Card" exists with: working title (≤60–70 chars, curiosity or extremity), thumbnail concept (one focal subject, ≤3–4 words of text, same promise as the title), hook (first 30 s), logline, 2–3 reasons it will work, and outlier evidence (≥1 comparable video at ≥2x its channel baseline, ideally ≥5x). This combines Galloway's one-pager, Abdaal's "title/thumbnail/hook first", and the vidIQ outlier bands.
- **Idea scoring rubric (suggested):** Outlier evidence (0–3: none / 2x / 5x / 10x+), Title-thumbnail promise coherence (0–2), Novelty vs the channel's last N formats (0–2; MrBeast "don't repeat formats back to back"), Payoff deliverability (0–2: can the video actually show what the package promises?), Creator-fit / first-hand angle (0–1). Comparison is pairwise: generate ≥2 packages per idea and have a human pick one, mirroring Galloway's side-by-side step.
- **Long-form script template (retention beats):** [0:00–0:30] hook that confirms the title/thumbnail promise immediately → [0:30–1:00] show, don't tell; front-load the best visual → [1–3 min] fast progression → [~3:00] re-engagement beat #1 → [3–6 min] simple, quick scene changes → [~6:00] re-engagement beat #2 (new info or a twist) → [6:00–end] keep the stakes; don't announce the ending; abrupt payoff. The agent should flag any section of 30–60 s+ with no new information, visual change, or stakes ("dull moment" lint).
- **Metric priority for post-publish analysis:** use watch-time-per-impression (CTR × AVD), not CTR alone, as the packaging KPI. This matches the A/B tool's watch-time-share winner rule and the memo's CTR+AVD pairing.
- **Policy guardrail:** an automation agent must avoid template-identical videos at scale. Each upload needs distinct substance (script, narrative, commentary). Same intro/outro is fine. AI script editing and unique AI backgrounds are explicitly OK. Compilations without a narrative are not.

## 2. Shorts / short-form (hooks, length, looping, long → short repurposing)

### Takeaway
Officially verified facts are structural: Shorts can be up to 3 minutes (since 2024-10-15), view counting changed on 2025-03-31 (a view counts on every play/replay, the old metric became "engaged views"), and the YPP "inauthentic content" rule penalizes templated, mass-produced Shorts. Hook/loop rules (1–3 s hook, loop ending into opening, re-hook every few seconds) are consistent across practitioners, but no official YouTube source for them was found.

### Cited Findings
- [SECONDARY reporting of YouTube's 2024-10 blog post] From 2024-10-15, Shorts can be up to 3 minutes for square or taller videos (previously 60 s). This was "a top requested feature by creators", and YouTube said it would improve recommendations for longer Shorts. — [PetaPixel 2024-10-04](https://petapixel.com/2024/10/04/youtube-shorts-expands-video-length-to-three-minutes/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=182474)
- [SECONDARY] From 2025-03-31, a Shorts view counts when the Short starts to play or replays, with no minimum watch time. The previous metric was renamed "engaged views", which still determine YPP eligibility. — [Tubefilter 2025-03-26](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/)
- [SECONDARY] vidIQ reports that from 2026-08-24 YouTube counts a public view from the first frame (Shorts included). Engaged views remain the comparison metric, and earnings/YPP are unaffected. — [vidIQ view-count update](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-view-count-update/)
- [PRIMARY] Templated mass production is out of policy (see §1): "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Secondary coverage singles out templated, repetitive Shorts formats. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [subsub.io (SECONDARY)](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2025)
- [SECONDARY practitioner consensus, not official] Hook within ~2–3 s (some guides say 1–5 s). Use a pattern interrupt or curiosity gap. Shorts loop by default, so end on a line or question the opening answers. Re-engage every 3–5 s with text, sound, or a new visual. Check the Shorts retention graph for a steep drop in the first 1–3 s. — [Opus Clip hook formulas](https://www.opus.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-hook-formulas); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/perfect-youtube-short-length-structure-hooks); [JoinBrands 2026](https://joinbrands.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices/); [Deeside 2026-01-30](https://www.deeside.com/the-shorts-recommendation-loop-a-practical-map-of-signals-you-can-actually-influence/)
- [SECONDARY, Korean guide] A Claude Code workflow guide shows extracting clips from long videos, writing per-platform descriptions and hashtags, and FFmpeg conversion scripts for each platform's specs. — [WikiDocs 클로드 코드 가이드 – 영상 편집 자동화](https://wikidocs.net/335635)

### Inferences
- **Short-form template:** [0–2 s] visual or verbal hook that states the payoff or tension (no logo, no intro) → [2–X s] one idea only, a re-hook every 3–5 s (caption change, cut, B-roll) → [last 1–2 s] loop bridge: the final line leads back into the first frame. Default length: under 60 s unless the story needs more; 3 min is the ceiling, not the target (an inference from engaged-views being the comparison metric).
- **Long → short repurposing checklist:** pick moments that are self-contained (make sense without context), have their own hook in the first 2 s, and deliver a payoff inside the clip. Re-frame to 9:16, burn in captions, write a new title rather than reusing the long-form title. Each Short must add distinct framing (commentary, caption narrative) to stay clear of the "inauthentic/reused" lines.
- **KPI rule:** after 2025-03-31 and 2026-08-24 the raw view count is inflated by replays, so the agent should compare Shorts on engaged views and retention (viewed vs swiped-away), not raw views.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube Creator Insider / YouTube Help page on Shorts hook timing, loops, or long→short best practice was retrieved. All hook/loop rules above are practitioner consensus.
- The official YouTube blog post for the 3-minute change and the official Help Community post for the view-count change were not fetched directly.
- Whether YouTube's recommender treats 60–180 s Shorts differently from <60 s Shorts in 2026 is unverified.

## 3. Writing systems (Justin Welsh, Dan Koe, Ship 30 atomic essays, Tiago Forte CODE, GaryVee content model)

### Takeaway
All of these share one pipeline shape: capture → distill into reusable atomic units → test small → expand winners → repurpose across platforms (hub and spoke / reverse pyramid). For an agent this maps to: an idea bank with scores, short-form "test" posts, promotion of the best performers to long-form, then automatic derivatives. Nearly all evidence retrieved here is secondhand.

### Cited Findings
- **Justin Welsh** [SECONDARY]: Content OS flow: Curate high-performing posts (Twitter/LinkedIn) → Templatize them into generic templates → Ideate → Distribute with automation. — [Vic Montano Substack](https://thevicmontano.substack.com/p/content-os-by-justin-welsh-an-automated)
- Justin Welsh [SECONDARY]: idea library built by brain-dumping subtopics → choosing 5–7 presentation styles (guides, predictions, counterarguments, stories, observations) → generating a 75–100-idea matrix (with ChatGPT) → tracking it in Notion with publish date, link, style, and a 1–10 score. He recycles top posts with slight updates; in one test two near-identical tweets posted 12 days apart both performed strongly. — [The Way Up](https://thewayup.beehiiv.com/p/how-justin-welsh-built-1-5m-followers-with-a-content-system); [5to9](https://5to9.beehiiv.com/p/building-3m-oneperson-content-business)
- Justin Welsh [SECONDARY podcast summary, 2022-12-06]: owning unique knowledge and repurposing through a "hub and spoke" model. — [Marketing Millennials ep.](https://themarketingmillennials.com/podcast/2022-12-06/ep-justin-welsh-content-systems-content-legends-virtual-series)
- Justin Welsh [SECONDARY]: flow of social posts (thread + LinkedIn post) → weekly newsletter built from the same material. — [Mango Network](https://themangonetwork.substack.com/p/the-repurpose-playbook-maximize-your)
- **Ship 30 for 30 atomic essays** [SECONDARY]: the term is credited to the Ship 30 creators. An atomic essay is one idea in ~250 words (about one page). Its purpose is to test audience interest before writing longer pieces. — [Kyoto/Synaps](https://kyoto.synaps.media/atomic-essays)
- Atomic essay template [SECONDARY Notion template, not an official Ship 30 doc]: ≤250 words; a short first sentence that states the "strong opinion"; then 3–7 main points, each with a short explanation. — [Notion: Writing Atomic Essays](https://heights.notion.site/Writing-Atomic-Essays-fdba76feddd94805b7c6bb4966faff39)
- **Tiago Forte CODE** [SECONDARY book summaries; book published 2022]: Capture (keep only what resonates, inspires, surprises, or helps a project), Organize (PARA: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives, sorted by actionability), Distill (progressive summarization: bold the key passages, then highlight the best of the bold), Express (the step everything else serves). "Intermediate packets" are reusable building blocks such as distilled notes, outtakes, and work in progress. An "archipelago of ideas" means starting from collected notes instead of a blank page. — [mmapped.blog](https://mmapped.blog/posts/16-building-a-second-brain); [Charter](https://www.charterworks.com/building-second-brain-tiago-forte/); [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/building-a-second-brain/136207)
- **GaryVee Content Model** [PRIMARY page exists on garyvaynerchuk.com, contents not fetched; details SECONDARY]: a "reverse pyramid". One long-form pillar (vlog, keynote, podcast, Q&A) is cut into micro-content (short videos, stills, carousels, text, blogs, audio bits) tailored per platform, and that micro-content points back to the pillar. Listen to the response, then repeat. "Document, don't create." Micro-content "must be created with purpose and not just to increase the content volume." — [GaryVee site](https://garyvaynerchuk.com/THE-GARYVEE-CONTENT-STRATEGY-HOW-TO-GROW-AND-DISTRIBUTE-YOUR-BRANDS-SOCIAL-MEDIA-CONTENT/); [Gist](https://getgist.com/gary-vee-content-model/); [Pepper Content](https://peppercontent.io/blog/garyvee-content-strategy)

### Inferences
- **Encodable "idea bank" schema** (Welsh + Forte): `idea_id, pillar/subtopic, style (guide | prediction | counterargument | story | observation | list), source_capture (link/note), first-hand angle (yes/no + note), score_1_10, status (captured → distilled → tested → expanded → repurposed), published_urls[], performance{}`.
- **Test-then-expand gate** (Ship 30 + Welsh): every pillar idea is first shipped as an atomic unit (≤250-word post / Short / LinkedIn post). Only units in roughly the top 20% by engagement get promoted to a long-form blog, newsletter, or YouTube script. The 20% threshold is a suggestion, not sourced.
- **Atomic unit lint:** one idea per unit; first sentence states a clear claim; 3–7 supporting points; ≤250 words; ends with a takeaway. Reject drafts with multiple theses.
- **Waterfall (GaryVee/hub-and-spoke) derivatives per pillar** — suggested defaults: 1 long-form video or blog → 3–5 Shorts/clips → 1 newsletter issue → 2–3 text posts (thread/LinkedIn/Threads) → 1 carousel/card-news set. Every derivative carries its own hook and points back to the pillar. Each must be reworded for the platform rather than copy-pasted, consistent with the "purpose, not volume" warning and with YouTube/Google anti-mass-production rules.
- **Recycling rule** (Welsh): the agent may resurface top performers after a cooldown with light updates. Track the last-posted date per idea.

## 4. SEO/blog in the AI era (Google gen-AI guidance, helpful content, E-E-A-T, scaled content abuse; Naver C-Rank / D.I.A.+ and Naver's AI stance)

### Takeaway
Google's position (docs updated 2026-08-28 to 2026-10-05) is explicit and encodable: AI is allowed for research and structure; mass-generating pages "without adding value for users" is scaled content abuse; all AI output, including titles, meta descriptions, alt text, and structured data, must be manually fact-checked before publishing; disclose "How" content was made where readers would expect it; never fabricate authors. For Naver, no official policy text on AI-generated posts was found in this session. The consistent secondhand reading is that AI assistance alone isn't penalized, but bulk near-duplicate posts and fake experience trigger low-quality (저품질) treatment, while first-hand experience, original photos, and channel-level trust (C-Rank) drive both ranking and AI Briefing citations.

### Cited Findings

**Google — generative AI guidance** [PRIMARY, last updated 2026-10-01]
- "Generative AI can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content." But "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate" the scaled content abuse policy. — [Google: Using gen-AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- It is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing". This applies to titles, meta descriptions, structured data, and image alt text too. Structured data must be validated. — [Google: Using gen-AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- Disclosure: "Sharing information about how a piece of content was created can help give your readers more context." For AI images use IPTC `DigitalSourceType` = `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia`. Merchant Center requires AI-generated product titles/descriptions to be labeled. — [Google: Using gen-AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- The page references Quality Rater Guidelines §4.6.5 (scaled content abuse) and §4.6.6 (main content with little effort, originality, or added value). Rater scores "don't directly influence ranking." — [Google: Using gen-AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)

**Google — spam policies** [PRIMARY, last updated 2026-08-28]
- "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings". Examples: gen-AI pages "without adding value for users"; pages with keywords that make little sense to readers; scraping or stitching content with little value. The spam policies also cover attempts to "manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search." — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- [SECONDARY] Google publishes no volume threshold. Volume alone is not a violation; purpose and value are what count. — [PPC Land: scaled content abuse](https://ppc.land/scaled-content-abuse/)

**Google — helpful, people-first content** [PRIMARY, last updated 2026-10-05]
- Self-assessment questions include: "Does the content provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?"; "Is this the sort of page you'd want to bookmark, share with a friend, or recommend?"; "Is the content mass-produced by or outsourced to a large number of creators...?"; "Does the content have any easily-verified factual errors?"; "Are you mainly summarizing what others have to say without adding much value?"; "Are you changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh when the content has not substantially changed?" — [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Who/How/Why. **Who**: a clear byline that links to author background. Fabricated creators (AI headshots, made-up names, false credentials) are deceptive. **How**: disclose automation/AI where a reader would reasonably ask, and explain why it was useful. **Why**: content should primarily help visitors. Automation used mainly to manipulate rankings violates spam policy. — [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- E-E-A-T: trust matters most. "E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor." YMYL topics (health, finance, safety) get more weight. — [Google: Creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

**Naver** [all SECONDARY; no official Naver Search Advisor / 네이버 서치 블로그 notice was retrieved]
- C-Rank is commonly described as creator/source reputation (출처 신뢰도) and D.I.A./D.I.A.+ as document quality and fit with search intent. ### Inferences
- **Pre-publish gate for every blog post (Google + Naver):**
  1. Fact-check pass. Every factual claim, number, or date needs a source link or a human confirmation flag. This also covers the title, meta description, alt text, and schema (Google says so explicitly).
  2. Value-add check. The post contains at least one of: first-hand experience, original data/screenshots/photos, original analysis, or an expert opinion. Pure "summary of others" fails (Google self-assessment question).
  3. Uniqueness check. Similarity to the agent's own previous posts must stay below a threshold; refuse to publish template-identical variants (scaled content abuse / Naver 저품질 risk).
  4. Author/Who. A real byline with a profile link. Never invent personas or experiences. Fake "I tried it" claims are a hard block.
  5. How/disclosure. Add a short AI-assist note where readers would expect one. Set IPTC `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia` on AI images.
  6. Freshness honesty. Don't bump dates without substantive changes.
  7. YMYL flag. Health, finance, or legal topics require human expert review.
- **Naver-specific template:** answer-first lead (the core answer in the first ~150 characters, for AI Briefing), then the experience section (real photos, dates, places), then structured body (subheads, lists, tables), then a summary. Keep a topical focus per blog to build C-Rank. Avoid ad-heavy density. Treat these as practitioner-derived heuristics, not Naver rules.
- **Cadence rule:** "many pages" is the abuse trigger on both platforms, so the agent should cap daily output per channel and require human approval for each post rather than batch auto-publishing.
